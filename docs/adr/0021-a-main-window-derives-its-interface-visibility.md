# A Main Window Derives Its Interface Visibility

Each **Main Window** derives its **Interface Visibility**, which of its bars show, their heights and whether the hover bar may show, as one record, `InterfaceVisibility`, from the three interface settings (interface, bottom bar, hover bar; ADR-0020) and the Window Mode it is shown in. A pure `InterfaceVisibility.For(showInterface, showBottomBar, showHoverBar, shownMode)` in Core holds the rule, in the shape of `GalleryAppearance.For` (ADR-0011) and `WindowModeState` (ADR-0006): fullscreen shows no interface and no bottom bar, both heights 0, and the hover bar when its setting is on; any other mode shows the interface as its setting says, the bottom bar only when the interface shows and its setting is on, the titlebar at `SizeDefaults.MainTitlebarHeight` when the interface shows and 0 otherwise, the bottom bar at `SizeDefaults.BottombarHeight` when it shows and 0 otherwise, and the hover bar when its setting is on and no bottom bar shows. Minimized derives from the mode the window comes back to, so minimizing a fullscreen window changes nothing. `MainWindowViewModel` combines the three published settings and its `WindowModeState` into one read-only `Interface`, and `IsUIShown`, `IsTopToolbarShown` (the same value under the name its bindings use), `IsBottomToolbarShown`, `TitlebarHeight`, `BottombarHeight` and `IsHoverBarShown` are read-only projections of it, so nothing outside the view model can write them. The three toggle Actions are one setting write each, the hover bar is the window's, bound through `ParentWindowContext`, and the Reserved Space reads the record.

Before this, seven hand-written entries in four modules wrote the window's shown state, 36 writes of six writable bindables: the five in `ToggleUIVisibility` (`ToggleBottomBar`, `ToggleInterface`, `ToggleHoverBar`, `RestoreInterface`, `FullscreenHideInterface`), `MainWindowLifecycle.ApplyWindowSettingsAsync` on the thread pool at every open, and `WindowModeSwitcher.Apply` around its native work, with a posted `FullscreenHideInterface` for a window that started fullscreen. They disagreed: `FullscreenHideInterface` never wrote the titlebar's height and `ToggleInterface` never zeroed it, so the fit reserved a hidden titlebar after a toggle and not after a hidden start. The hover bar was per tab, a `HoverbarViewModel` written for the active tab only by six writers: the toggles, every load through `SourceOpener.ShowHoverBarIfNeeded` with a rule that hid what `ToggleBottomBar` had just shown, the gallery through `GalleryAppearance.ShowsHoverBar` on every mode or dock change, and `HoverFadeButtonHandler` on pointer enter, so a tab switched to showed the state of its own last write. None of the seven had a test.

## Considered Options

- **The record in Core and an applier in Avalonia** that writes the six writable bindables, the architecture review's literal shape. One more module, the bindables stay writable so a seventh writer can come back, and its tests need a headless window. Nothing in the rule needs Avalonia. Chosen instead: the view model derives read-only bindables.
- **Six separate derivations, no record.** Fewer types, but the rule is spread over six expressions again, cannot be tested as one table, and the Reserved Space would read six things instead of one.
- **The fade-buttons setting as a fourth input**, as a load and the hover handler used it. It would have to publish, and it gates whether anything fades in on hover, which the handler already does; the record says only whether the hover bar may show.
- **The Expanded Gallery hides the hover bar**, as the Gallery Appearance said. The gallery is a tab's; the hover bar's rule is the window's, and an input from the active tab would make the record follow tab switches for one field.
- **The hover bar stays per tab**, written for every tab by the window. Every tab would hold the same value, and a tab moved to another window would need a write on arrival; one window bindable reached through `ParentWindowContext` needs neither.
- **`IsUIShown` and `IsTopToolbarShown` merged into one name.** They are one value, but the merge is about 20 binding edits across both platforms for no change in behaviour; one property under two names costs nothing.
- **`For` takes `WindowModeState`** instead of the shown mode. It would read one private member of the state; passing the mode keeps `For` a function of four values, and `Shown` public is one line.

## Consequences

- The six bindables are read-only, and a write outside the view model does not compile. What reacts to a bar subscribes to `Interface` or to one projection; the fitter follows `Interface` alone.
- A Window Mode change, a load and the gallery no longer touch the interface. The record follows the `WindowModeState` the switcher publishes, so a window that starts fullscreen derives hidden bars before its first frame, and no start-up write or posted hide is needed. `ToggleUIVisibility` is deleted.
- The record has value equality: a setting toggled while fullscreen notifies nothing and posts no fit, and the bar shows once the window leaves fullscreen.
- The hover bar is the window's, reached through `ParentWindowContext.IsHoverBarShown`, so a tab moved to another Main Window follows that window's. `HoverbarViewModel` is deleted, and the Gallery Appearance no longer says whether the hover bar shows.
- The Reserved Space reads the record, `ReservedSpace.For(tabCount, visibility, galleryWidth, galleryHeight)`. With several tabs and the interface hidden it reserves the tab strip without the collapsed titlebar, which the toggle used to reserve.
- The fade-buttons setting gates only the fade-in, in `HoverFadeButtonHandler`; a hidden hover bar receives no pointer.
- The bottom bar sizes its own buttons when it becomes visible, from its top level's width, in place of the two posts the toggles made.
- `IsTopToolbarShown` is `IsUIShown` under a second name, kept for its roughly 40 bindings. The one `IsChecked` binding on a bar, the bottom bar menu item, is `OneWay`.
- The three interface settings publish their value (ADR-0020), and the window's shown state is derived from them; every Main Window derives from the same three and differs only by its Window Mode.
