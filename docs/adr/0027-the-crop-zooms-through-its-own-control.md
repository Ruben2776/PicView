# The Crop Zooms Through Its Own Control

A **Tab**'s crop view zooms and pans as its image viewer does, through a `ZoomPanControl` of its own, so the user can place the selection's edges on exact pixels. `ZoomPanControl` is told which view it zooms by its `Host`, a `ZoomPanHost` of `ImageViewer` or `Crop`, set in XAML, and the host decides when a press pans, in one function, `StartsPan`. In the viewer nothing changed: a left press pans a zoomed image, a press with Shift alone is left for moving the window, and a double-click resets when the setting says so. In the crop a left press pans only while Space is held, since a plain press moves or resizes the selection; the press is then marked handled, Shift keeps its meaning as the square-resize modifier, and there is no double-click reset. The key is fixed to Space for now. The control follows it on its window, with the tunnel strategy and handled keys included, and forgets it when the window is deactivated, the crop's context menu opens or the control leaves the tree, since a window that lost the keyboard does not see the key released. While Space is held `CropControl` turns off hit testing on its selection canvas, so no move or resize can start and the pan cursor shows over the handles.

`CropControl` is as large as the view, the window's `ScrollViewerWidth` and `ScrollViewerHeight`, and centres the crop surface, the fitted image with the selection drawn over it, inside its `ZoomPanControl`, so a zoomed crop has the room the viewer gives a zoomed image. The panel that is zoomed is at least as large as the view and as large as an image fitted larger than the view, as a tall image is with scrolling on, so all of such an image can be zoomed and panned to; it then starts at the view's top left. The eight handles, the selection's border and the size label keep their size on screen: on each `ScaleChanged` the control scales them by one over the scale, the handles about their centres and the label from its bottom left, and sets the border's thickness to 2 over the scale. `CropLayoutManager` places the handles and the label inside the part of the image the view shows, measured at the size they are drawn on screen, so the label stops at the top of the view as it stops at the top of the image. A corner's handle shows only while its corner is in view, and a side's handle slides along its side to stay in view and shows only while that side is in view: a handle drawn at the view's edge for an edge that is out of view would not sit on the edge it moves. The crop's zoom is never animated, because `ScaleChanged` reports the target scale at once and the overlay would jump while the image glided.

Crop stays a **Blocking Input Mode**, but it handles three ordinary Actions: `ZoomIn`, `ZoomOut` and `ResetZoom`. `InputMode.Allows` names them and `Keymap.Resolve` lets a blocking mode run only what it allows, so wheel navigation and every other Keybind still do nothing while cropping. `CropControl` is a second `IZoomScrollTarget` and sends its wheel through `KeybindRouter.WheelAsync`, so the user's wheel settings decide whether a wheel step zooms.

The zoom is carried from the viewer to the crop by the adapter that owns them both, `TabViews.Set` (ADR-0022). What it carries is a `ZoomView`: the scale, and the point at the centre of the view as a fraction of the image. When the tab's kind becomes Crop it reads the viewer's view and makes the crop control with it; the crop shows it after its first layout and places its default selection in the middle of the part of the image the view shows. Nothing is carried back: zooming in a crop is for placing the selection, so when the kind leaves Crop the viewer comes back as it was left, and the adapter writes the viewer's scale to the tab's Zoom Factor, which the crop wrote while it was shown. When the tab shows another image than the one the crop opened on, as after a crop saved over the shown file, the adapter first resets the viewer's zoom when zoom resets on image change, since the viewer was not shown when the new image's fit reset the shown view. The viewer's `ZoomPanControl` shows its zoom preview on attach only when it is zoomed, so a viewer that comes back unzoomed shows none.

The selection is held in image pixels. `CropViewModel` holds a whole-pixel rectangle, `PixelX`, `PixelY`, `PixelSelectionWidth` and `PixelSelectionHeight`, as the one truth, and derives the values the surface draws, `SelectionX`, `SelectionY`, `SelectionWidth` and `SelectionHeight`, as the pixels times the tab's Fit Scale. `SetSelection` is the only way in: it takes a rectangle in view units, rounds each edge to a whole pixel, keeps the rectangle inside the image and at least one pixel each way, and derives the view values again, so the drawn border sits on pixel edges and steps from one pixel to the next when zoomed in. `Refit` derives them again when the Fit Scale changed, which `CropControl` calls when the window's fitted size changes, so the selection stays on the same pixels through a resize.

Before this, the crop could not zoom: the wheel handler lived on the viewer, which is not in the tree during a crop, and Crop blocked the zoom Actions. The selection was held as whole numbers of view units and converted to pixels at the end, in three places in `CropService`; on an image fitted at a quarter of its size one step of the selection was four pixels, and a rounded origin plus a rounded size could reach one pixel past the image. The drag and resize handlers wrote the rectangle's canvas position themselves. `ZoomPanControl.Initialize` required a `ZoomPreviewer`, and added the pointer handlers once while every detach removed them, so the kept viewer of a tab would not pan again after its crop closed.

## Considered Options

- **The crop as an overlay inside the image viewer**, on the viewer's own `ZoomPanControl`, which would only switch how it pans. The zoom would carry over for free and there would be one zoom state. But Crop would stop being its own Tab View, reversing ADR-0022, and the viewer's arrows, hover bar, gallery and click behaviour would each have to stand down while cropping.
- **The view position on `TabZoom`**, beside the Zoom Factor, read and written by both controls. A pan would write to Core on every pointer move, and ADR-0016 gave `TabZoom` two inputs on purpose.
- **Raw translations handed across** instead of a fraction of the image. The gallery hides for a crop, so the view area and the fitted size differ between the two views and the translations would not line up.
- **The crop's view handed back to the viewer** when the crop closes, which was built first. A crop opened unzoomed and zoomed in to place an edge left the viewer zoomed in with its zoom preview showing, when the user had only meant to crop.
- **Handles that grow with the image**, the whole surface inside the transform and nothing countered. At four times zoom the handles would be 80 wide and the border 8, over the edge the user is placing.
- **Animated zoom in the crop**, with the overlay following the rendered scale each frame as the viewer's motion photo badges do. A per-frame poll for an effect the crop does not need.
- **A pan gesture named for what it does**, such as a `PanGesture` of `Drag` or `SpaceDrag`, instead of a host. The host also decides the Shift rule, the double-click reset and the animation, which a gesture name would not cover.
- **A notification on `TabZoom` for a Fit Scale change**, so `CropViewModel` could refit itself. The fitted size already notifies in the window's view model, where the crop control is, and `TabZoom` keeps to ADR-0016.

## Consequences

- `ZoomPanControl` has two hosts. A third names itself in `ZoomPanHost` and its rule in `StartsPan`, which is tested as a table.
- `Initialize` takes an optional `ZoomPreviewer`; the crop has none. The pointer handlers are added on every attach and removed on every detach.
- ADR-0016's Zoom Factor has the shown view's control as its writer, the viewer's or the crop's, and the adapter writes it once when a crop ends. The title shows the Cropping phase meanwhile (ADR-0015).
- ADR-0022 holds: Crop is a Tab View with a control made per crop. The adapter now gives that control the viewer's view, and keeps the model the crop opened on to tell a changed image.
- The crop control no longer binds its size to `ImageWidth` and `ImageHeight`, as ADR-0022 and ADR-0026 describe; its crop surface does.
- Nothing outside `CropViewModel` writes the selection, and `CropService` reads the pixel rectangle in its three places. `CropLayoutManager` places the rectangle, the handles and the dimming from the derived values.
- The dimming is one shape, the image with the selection as its hole, and the selection canvas is not layout-rounded. Four dimming rectangles side by side had been rounded to whole view units to hide the seams where they met; a selection on image pixels lies at fractions of a unit, where the seams came back and layout rounding moved the border off the pixels it marks. One shape has no joins, so nothing is rounded.
- The selection stays in the part of the image the view shows, which is all of it until the crop is zoomed in. That part is drawn in to whole image pixels first, so an edge that sits on a pixel never comes to lie outside the view. A move stops at its edges, a resize holds the dragged edge to it, and after a zoom or a pan, which `ZoomPanControl` reports through `ViewChanged`, the crop control moves the selection back into it. A selection longer than the view on an axis cannot fit, so on that axis it keeps to the image and is left where it is; an edge already off screen stays put while another edge is resized.
- `RotationManager.ResetZoom` resets the crop's zoom when the active tab shows a crop.
- The input hot path allocates nothing more: `Allows` and `StartsPan` are comparisons.
